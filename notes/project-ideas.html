🎯 Project Selection Framework & Evaluation Criteria

To ensure these projects provide lasting intellectual leverage in the age of artificial intelligence, every proposed project satisfies five strict criteria:

  1. Semester Feasibility: Scoped to be independently designed, executed, analyzed, and written up within a standard 12-week academic term by an individual student.
  2. Curricular Grounding: Directly anchors and deepens the ASTB23 syllabus—leveraging hydrostatic equilibrium, Lane-Emden polytropes, degenerate Fermi gas physics, Chandrasekhar limits, epicyclic frequency theory, Jeans’ theorem, or galactic rotation curve decomposition.
  3. AI Automation Resilience: Focuses on epistemic skills that statistical AI systems cannot reliably automate: resolving singular coordinate boundary conditions, navigating non-trivial observational selection effects, proving numerical stability on curved phase spaces, and performing causal model selection.
  4. Academic Leverage (Impressing Faculty): Formulated with the mathematical and physical rigor required to impress course professors (e.g., Prof. Pawel Artymowicz) and serve as a springboard for undergraduate research awards (e.g., NSERC USRA, URECs) or conference posters.
  5. Industry & Graduate Value: Cultivates computational tools (Bayesian MCMC, symplectic orbit integrators, physics-informed numerical methods) highly prized in quantitative finance, aerospace engineering, machine learning research, and top astrophysics graduate admissions.

🌌 Project Option 1: Relativistic Fermi-Dirac Polytrope Solver with High-Order Singular Boundary Resolution

1. Context & Motivation (ASTB23 Weeks 2–6)

The classical Lane-Emden equation models self-gravitating polytropic gas spheres: \[\frac{1}{\xi^2}\frac{d}{d\xi}\left(\xi^2 \frac{d\theta}{d\xi}\right) = -\theta^n\] with central boundary conditions \(\theta(0) = 1\) and \(\theta'(0) = 0\). In white dwarfs (Chandrasekhar theory, Lectures 11–12), stars transition from non-relativistic degenerate electrons (\(n = 1.5\), \(P \propto \rho^{5/3}\)) in low-mass regimes to ultra-relativistic degeneracy (\(n = 3\), \(P \propto \rho^{4/3}\)) as central density approaches the Chandrasekhar mass boundary (\(M \to M_{\text{Ch}} \approx 1.44 M_\odot\)).

Standard language models fail when generating numerical solvers for this system because the origin \(\xi = 0\) is a coordinate singularity where \(\frac{2}{\xi} \frac{d\theta}{d\xi} \to \frac{0}{0}\).

2. Concrete Deliverables & Scope

  • Analytical Series Expansion: Derive the 6th-order Taylor series for \(\theta(\xi)\) around \(\xi = 0\) to initiate numerical integration off the singular origin: \[\theta(\xi) \approx 1 - \frac{\xi^2}{6} + \frac{n}{120}\xi^4 - \frac{n(8n - 5)}{15120}\xi^6 + \mathcal{O}(\xi^8)\]
  • General Relativistic Correction: Implement the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) relativistic hydrostatic equilibrium equation alongside the exact Fermi-Dirac integral equation of state across density shells from \(10^5 \text{ g/cm}^3\) to \(10^{10} \text{ g/cm}^3\).
  • Adaptive Shooting Integrator: Build an adaptive 4th/5th-order Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg (RK45) shooting solver in Python that integrates from \(\xi_0 = 10^{-4}\) to the first root \(\xi_1\) where \(\theta(\xi_1) = 0\).
  • Mass-Radius Curve Benchmark: Reproduce the exact Chandrasekhar mass-radius relation \(M(R)\), explicitly pinpointing where relativistic instability triggers gravitational collapse.

3. Why This Impresses Professors & Employers

  • Demonstrates mastery of asymptotic expansions and numerical analysis for singular differential equations.
  • Shows deep comprehension of quantum statistical mechanics (Fermi-Dirac degeneracy) coupled with general relativistic hydrostatic equilibrium.
  • Employers in quantitative finance and scientific computing prize researchers who can write unconditionally robust ODE solvers rather than relying on black-box numerical libraries.

🌀 Project Option 2: Bayesian MCMC Rotation Curve Decomposition & Dark Matter Halo Cusp-vs-Core Discriminator with Real Gaia/SPARC Data

1. Context & Motivation (ASTB23 Weeks 9–11)

In galactic dynamics, the observed circular velocity curve \(v_{\text{circ}}(R)\) reflects the gravitational contribution of multiple distinct components: \[v_{\text{circ}}^2(R) = v_{\text{gas}}^2(R) + \Upsilon_{\text{disk}} v_{\text{disk}}^2(R) + \Upsilon_{\text{bulge}} v_{\text{bulge}}^2(R) + v_{\text{DM}}^2(R)\] where \(\Upsilon\) denotes the stellar mass-to-light ratio.

A central unsolved debate in astrophysics is the “Cusp-vs-Core Problem”: standard cold dark matter (CDM) cosmological simulations predict cuspy central density profiles (Navarro-Frenk-White: \(\rho_{\text{NFW}}(r) \propto r^{-1}\) as \(r \to 0\)), whereas observed rotation curves of low-surface-brightness (LSB) galaxies frequently prefer constant-density cored profiles (Burkert: \(\rho_{\text{Burkert}}(r) \to \text{const}\)).

2. Concrete Deliverables & Scope

  • Observational Pipeline: Ingest calibrated rotation curve data from the open-access SPARC database (175 late-type galaxies) and local solar neighborhood kinematics from Gaia DR3.
  • Analytical Potential Library: Implement analytical velocity profiles for:
    • Exponential Freeman thin disk
    • Hernquist / Plummer spherical stellar bulge
    • NFW cuspy dark matter halo
    • Burkert cored dark matter halo
  • Bayesian Nested Sampling / MCMC: Using emcee or dynesty, estimate joint posterior distributions for halo parameters (concentration \(c\), virial mass \(M_{200}\), core radius \(r_0\)) and stellar mass-to-light ratios \((\Upsilon_{\text{disk}}, \Upsilon_{\text{bulge}})\).
  • Model Selection: Compute the marginal likelihood (Bayesian Evidence \(\mathcal{Z}\)) and Bayes factor \(\ln B_{\text{core, cusp}}\) across dwarf vs giant galaxies to statistically quantify where cuspy halos fail.

3. Why This Impresses Professors & Employers

  • Moves beyond simplified textbook toy models to real astronomical data wrangling with observational uncertainties and degeneracies.
  • Establishes rigorous Bayesian model comparison skills—the exact mathematical framework used in quantitative trading, actuarial risk analysis, and state-of-the-art machine learning.

🪐 Project Option 3: Symplectic Orbital Resonance & Chaos Mapper in Non-Axisymmetric Galactic Potentials

1. Context & Motivation (ASTB23 Weeks 10–12)

Stellar orbits in non-axisymmetric galactic potentials (such as barred spiral galaxies) do not conserve angular momentum \(L_z\). Instead, in a coordinate frame corotating with bar pattern speed \(\Omega_b\), the conserved quantity is the Jacobi integral: \[E_J = \frac{1}{2}v^2 + \Phi(\mathbf{x}) - \frac{1}{2}\Omega_b^2 (x^2 + y^2)\] Resonances between epicyclic radial frequency \(\kappa\) and orbital frequency \(\Omega\) create the Inner Lindblad Resonance (ILR, \(\Omega - \kappa/2 = \Omega_b\)) and Outer Lindblad Resonance (OLR, \(\Omega + \kappa/2 = \Omega_b\)).

2. Concrete Deliverables & Scope

  • Symplectic Integrator: Construct a 4th-order symplectic Ruth-Forest or Yoshida integrator in Python or C++ that preserves the Jacobi integral \(E_J\) to \(10^{-8}\) precision over \(10^4\) orbital periods.
  • Bar Potential Model: Combine a Miyamoto-Nagai disk potential with a prolate Ferrers bar potential.
  • Poincaré Surfaces of Section: Generate \((y, \dot{y})\) Poincaré puncture maps at \(x = 0\) across diverse Jacobi energy regimes.
  • Lyapunov Chaos Diagnostics: Calculate maximum Lyapunov exponents via two-trajectory phase-space divergence to map out islands of regular orbits vs chaotic seas.

3. Why This Impresses Professors & Employers

  • Masterfully bridges Taylor classical analytical dynamics with modern astrophysics research.
  • Demonstrates symplectic computing and non-linear dynamical systems theory, crucial for orbital mechanics, satellite constellation mission design, and robotics.
